### Outstanding Content I Greatly Appreciated

#### Talk 1 – What does a DevOps engineer actually do?
*Speaker: Mr. Trong H. Truong – DevOps Engineer at Endava Vietnam*

*   **Busting myths:** Mr. Trong helped me realize that DevOps isn't just about "typing a few CI/CD commands" or "being a superhero waking up at midnight to restart Kubernetes". The essence of DevOps lies in **systems thinking**, not just manual labor.
*   **Where to start:** His hard-earned advice is to build a rock-solid core foundation before chasing flashy tools. Must-have skills include:
    *   **Foundation:** Must master the Linux OS and Networking.
    *   **Programming:** Should be proficient in Python or Golang.
    *   **Core tools:** Source code management with Git, understanding CI/CD flows and Container architectures.
*   **Application lifecycle:** A DevOps engineer must fully understand how an application is built, tested, deployed, logged, and configured.
*   **Valuable lessons I learned:**
    *   *Just because you copy/paste a command from the internet and it works doesn't mean you understand it.*
    *   *Always ask "why" a system works that way before asking "how" to set it up.*
    *   *Don't try to be a superhero fixing errors manually; automate the boring tasks and make the workflow transparent to everyone on the team.*

#### Talk 2 – From First Cloud AI Journey to AWS Partner
*Speaker: Mr. Danh Hoang Hieu Nghi – AI Engineer, AWS Community Builder, AWS Student Builder Group Leader*

*   **Looking further:** Mr. Nghi shared that the first job is just the starting point. From a Cloud foundation, we can completely branch out to become a Solutions Architect, DevOps Engineer, Platform Engineer, or Software Engineer.
*   **Benefits from the AWS community:** He introduced excellent launchpads to accelerate careers:
    *   Joining the **AWS Student Builder Group Program** (formerly AWS Cloud Clubs).
    *   Becoming an **AWS Community Builder**.
    *   Grasping opportunities within the **AWS Partner** network.

#### Talk 3 – Data Analytics Engineer: Real stories and MNC culture
*Speakers: Mr. Cuong Nguyen & Mr. Dat Pham*

*   **The truth about the Data industry:** A Data career isn't just about throwing a bunch of lifeless charts at your boss; the real value lies in finding the *reasons* behind those numbers (e.g., why GMV dropped this month) and the ability to "tell a story" using data (Data Storytelling).
*   **Soft skills are a secret weapon:** Critical thinking, problem-solving, and clear communication are just as vital as writing good SQL.
*   **Unveiling the MNC recruitment process:**
    *   Round 1: CV screening via ATS and an English pre-interview with HR.
    *   Round 2: Competency test (Logic, algorithms, or real-world situational handling).
    *   Round 3: In-depth technical interview (Often using the STAR method to dissect a candidate's experience).
    *   Round 4: Culture Fit assessment interview with senior management.
*   **Decoding "Corporate Culture":**
    *   *No-Blame Post-Mortem culture (common in Tech MNCs):* When a system crashes, people sit together to find where the process failed to fix it, rather than looking for someone to blame.
    *   *Caring & Inclusive culture (in FMCG MNCs):* A human-centric environment that deeply respects diversity.
*   **Global standards:** Borrowing historical stories (Japan's Wakon Yosai spirit or South Korea's Chaebol model), they emphasized that adhering to strict international standards is the only way to reach a global level.

### What I Accumulated

#### Technical & Career Strategy

*   **Build a solid foundation:** Mastering how the Linux OS works, deeply understanding networking, and being fluent in a language (like Python or Golang) is more important than chasing tool trends. As they said: "tools will become obsolete, but foundations last forever".
*   **AWS Ecosystem:** Actively participating in AWS community programs not only helps improve knowledge but also expands professional networks very quickly.
*   **Data Analytics Mindset:** Coding skills must always go hand in hand with a business mindset. An excellent data engineer knows how to turn dry numbers into valuable insights for the business.

#### Culture & Mindset

*   **Culture Fit:** Multinational companies might hire you for your skills, but they are willing to fire you if you don't fit the culture. Internalizing the *No-Blame Post-Mortem* ideology is mandatory baggage to survive in high-quality tech teams.
*   **Systems thinking in DevOps:** Look at the big picture of the software architecture and always find ways to automate to free up manual labor.
*   **Practice is truth:** The common advice from all speakers is to manually deploy a project, intentionally break it, and then figure out how to fix it yourself — that's the fastest way to learn Cloud.

---

### Plan to Apply in Reality

*   **Strengthen foundations:** I will spend more time digging deeper into the Linux bash shell and basic networking concepts (OSI, TCP/IP) before jumping into Kubernetes or other complex systems.
*   **Practice interview skills:** Start systematizing personal projects and practice answering interviews strictly according to the STAR structure: Situation -> Task -> Action -> Result.
*   **Immerse in the community:** Plan to participate more actively in the AWS Student Builder Group to network and learn.
*   **Practice "No-Blame" mindset:** Apply this mindset immediately to school group projects; when a bug occurs, the whole team will look at the code to debug instead of arguing about who wrote that piece of code.
